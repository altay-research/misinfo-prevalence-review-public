SCREENING CRITERIA v2 (2026-09-02) — title-stage RE-SCREEN of records excluded in June 2026.
Supersedes screening_criteria.txt for this re-screen. The June criteria omitted self-reported
recall and were read as "observed behaviour only"; both omissions are corrected here.

Review: the PREVALENCE, EXPOSURE, SHARING and CONCENTRATION of misinformation. "Misinformation"
is broad: misinformation, disinformation, fake/false news, unreliable/untrustworthy sources or
websites, junk/low-quality news, inaccurate or misleading health content, conspiracy theories.

Assign one label per TITLE, using judgment (do not keyword-match):

INCLUDE — the title indicates a study that plausibly reports a quantitative share, with a
denominator, of any of SIX constructs:
  1. SELF-REPORTED RECALL: share of survey respondents who say they encountered, came across,
     were exposed to, received, or shared misinformation (any wording, any window), including
     "perceived exposure" or "perceived amount" of misinformation. Surveys of "fake news sharing",
     "exposure to misinformation", "experience of fake news" COUNT even when the title frames
     motivations, literacy, hesitancy or predictors.
  2. CONTENT PREVALENCE: share of items in a sample (posts, videos, comments, articles, websites,
     search results, chatbot answers, ads) classified as false, misleading, inaccurate, unreliable,
     conspiratorial or misinformation. "Content analysis of misinformation on <platform>",
     "accuracy of <topic> videos", "misinformation in <topic> content" COUNT.
  3. AUDIENCE EXPOSURE (share of what people saw/visited that was misinformation, from logs/panels).
  4. REACH (share of people who encountered it at least once, behavioural data).
  5. OBSERVED SHARING (share of shares/retweets/posts/links carrying misinformation, or of users who
     shared it, platform data).
  6. CONCENTRATION (top users/sources accounting for a share of exposure or sharing; supersharers).

MAYBE — plausibly one of the six but the title is ambiguous, or you are unsure.
  >>> When in doubt choose MAYBE; never EXCLUDE a possibly relevant study. <<<

EXCLUDE — only if the title is CLEARLY: detection/classification/algorithm method; intervention,
correction, inoculation or fact-checking-effect study; belief in or susceptibility to specific
false claims only (no exposure or sharing measure); hypothetical sharing intentions or "would you
share" experiments; non-empirical (commentary, theory, book, editorial, proceedings, dataset paper);
quality-score-only (DISCERN/GQS) with no accuracy coding; clearly off-topic.

Output per record: label (INCLUDE/MAYBE/EXCLUDE) + reason of <= 12 words.
