# Corpus repair, September 2026 — what was wrong, what we did, what it cost

Audience: a reviewer, an editor, or a future reader asking whether this review's corpus is sound.
Everything here is reproducible from the scripts named; every count comes from a file in the repo.
Nothing in this document is an estimate of what we think happened — the numbers are recomputed by
`scripts/merge_flagged_adjudication.py` and `scripts/merge_repair_extractions.py` on every run.

## 1. The defect

The screening criteria written in June 2026 (`docs/screening_criteria.txt`,
`docs/screening_criteria_stage2.txt`) enumerated the eligible constructs as audience exposure,
consumption, observed sharing, concentration, actor-level prevalence and content prevalence. They
did **not** name self-reported recall, and they listed "belief/susceptibility/perception-only" as an
exclusion ground. Self-reported recall — the share of survey respondents who say they encountered or
shared misinformation — is one of the review's six analysed constructs. It was therefore an
instructed exclusion at both the title and the abstract stage.

The 27 recall studies in the June corpus entered *despite* the criteria, not because of them:
screeners advanced them on the strength of the review's title and topic. That is the signature of
the defect. Content prevalence was also under-specified: the criteria named it but did not say that
single-keyword, most-viewed or rank-truncated samples qualify, so an unknown share of content
analyses were dropped on a stricter reading than the protocol intended.

The defect was found on 2026-09-02, during the round-3 human validation, not by a reviewer. Two of
40 sampled abstract-stage exclusions turned out to be eligible studies both human coders would have
advanced. A same-model false-negative audit run before the freeze had passed both. That is why the
repair below uses independent model families rather than another pass by the same one.

## 2. The repair, stage by stage

Corrected criteria: `data/extract_v2/qa/flagged_abstracts/adjudication/CRITERIA.md`, which names all
six constructs explicitly and states the content-prevalence sampling rules and the
quality-versus-content boundary.

### Stage 1 — the 1,120 abstract-stage exclusions
Two independent screeners re-read every abstract-stage exclusion under the corrected criteria:
Claude (Anthropic) and gpt-5.6-luna (OpenAI, run by the author in Codex), each blind, one batch of
100 per fresh session. Claude flagged 185, GPT 137, agreement 87%; both caught both known misses.
Union 233 → adjudicated → 66 eligible, 98 unsure, 69 not eligible → 162 sent to retrieval.

### Stage 2 — the 17,583 title-stage exclusions
The same two families re-screened every title-stage exclusion (47 batches each). Claude flagged
2,499 (14.2%), GPT 2,056 (11.7%); flag agreement 87.5%. Union 3,379 titles.
Abstracts were then fetched for those 3,379 (OpenAlex 1,502; Semantic Scholar 1,041; Crossref and
PubMed a further 67 later), leaving 473 title-only. An abstract-stage screen flagged 1,645, which
were adjudicated in 18 slices under the corrected criteria: **315 eligible, 745 unsure, 585 not
eligible**. Among the eligible, the expected construct was recall for 178 and content for 119 —
the two constructs the defect touched, which is the diagnostic confirmation.

A note on the 981 garbled title records: the June title file had a leaked-reason field for 981 rows.
774 were recovered from the corpus and re-shipped as repair batches; the repair decision supersedes
the "title missing" decision. `data/extract_v2/qa/title_rescreen/repair_map.csv` records which.

### Retrieval
Automated first, in this order: the pipeline's own PDF stores, the author's reference library matched
by DOI, OpenAlex open access, Unpaywall, Europe PMC. That yielded 54 of 162 at stage 1 and 109 of
315 at stage 2. OpenAlex's free daily budget was exhausted mid-run on 2026-09-03; the script gained
back-off and the Unpaywall and Europe PMC fallbacks, and the affected records were retried after the
reset. The remaining 314 went to manual institutional retrieval, of which the author obtained 172.
**142 remain unretrieved** and are listed, with the estimate each is expected to hold, in
`docs/RA_package/papers_to_retrieve.html` (generated by `scripts/build_retrieval_page.py`).

Three retrieved files turned out not to be the paper: a STROBE checklist supplement, a REDCap coding
form, and an accepted manuscript whose numbers differ from the published version. All three were
re-retrieved from Europe PMC and re-extracted. This is logged because it is exactly the kind of
silent error that a text-only pipeline can carry into a dataset.

### Full-text screening and extraction
Every retrieved paper was screened at full text and, if eligible, extracted to the frozen dataset's
schema by an Opus-class agent reading one paper at a time, four papers per session, under
`docs/repair_extraction_2026-09/INSTRUCTIONS.md`. Each row carries a verbatim source quote, and
`scripts/validate_repair_extractions.py` checks schema, controlled vocabularies, construct-specific
requirements and that each quote's opening is found verbatim in the paper's text.

## 3. What the repair yields (campaign complete: 335 of 335 retrieved papers extracted)

| | count |
|---|---|
| Papers screened at full text | 335 |
| Included | 208 |
| Excluded | 127 |
| Estimate rows | 554, from 208 studies |
| Rows flagged borderline | 179 |
| Rows entering the freeze (`pool = main`) | 314, from 147 studies |
| Rows held in the appendix pool | 219 |
| Rows dropped | 21 |

Source: `data/extract_v2/repair_2026-09/repair_summary.md` and `repair_estimates_final.csv`, which
carries a `pool` and a `pool_reason` for every row. The main/appendix/drop split is the effect of
the author's class-level rulings R1-R10 (`rulings_effect.md`) and of the wave-2 dispute
adjudication that followed (§5).

**The over-call.** Of papers an adjudicator judged ELIGIBLE from the abstract, 32% (86 of 266) do
not survive the full text. Of those judged UNSURE, 41% (28 of 69) do. The review must therefore
report the full-text-confirmed count, never the adjudicated count, and both are given here.

**Why 127 papers failed at full text.** In almost every case the construct is present but no share is
reported. The paper gives a Likert or 0-10 scale mean, or a latent construct with only path
coefficients, or a value that exists solely in an unlabelled figure, or it defers descriptives to a
supplement or OSF appendix. Abstract-stage screening cannot see any of this, which is the honest
explanation for the over-call and is itself a finding worth stating: a large class of survey studies
measure self-reported exposure and never publish a prevalence.

## 4. Effect on the review's numbers (study-level medians, the review's own pooling)

Computed on the released freeze v1.7.16, splitting its studies by where they came from. The middle
column is the June corpus as it survives in v1.7.16, so it differs slightly from the v1.7.14 figures
quoted while the repair was in progress (the wave-2 adjudication removed six studies).

| construct | studies the repair added | June-corpus studies | released freeze v1.7.16 |
|---|---|---|---|
| CONTENT | 21.5 (k=65) | 23.5 (k=210) | 23.2 (k=275) |
| RECALL | 40.6 (k=67) | 53.3 (k=27) | 41.0 (k=94) |
| SHARING | 23.0 (k=11) | 9.6 (k=30) | 12.4 (k=41) |

Content does not move. **Recall falls by roughly twelve points and more than triples its study
count** — the single substantive consequence of the repair, and it narrows the exposure-perception
gap the Discussion builds on. In the freeze, recall is reported split by what the respondent is
asked about: seen-recall 56.2% (k=75) and shared-recall 19.5% (k=33), pooled 41.0% (k=94).

Sharing rises, and the reason is a denominator effect rather than a platform or measurement-mode
effect: on Twitter alone the repair's median is 23.0 against the June corpus's 8.2. The repair's
sharing studies are mostly shares of links inside a keyword-topical corpus, while the June ones are
shares of a political-news or whole-diet stream. That is the contrast the review exists to
document, so sharing is reported stratified by denominator rather than as one pooled figure
(`data/extract_v2/repair_2026-09/POST_CAMPAIGN_CHECKS.md`, "the two thin cells").

## 5. Independent verification of the repair itself

A different model family (OpenAI gpt-5.6-luna, run in Codex) re-coded the repair's rows in two
waves. Each batch was coded twice in separate sessions — blind first, where the construct is coded
from the reported quantity with our code hidden, then fact-check mode against the paper. Scored by
`scripts/score_gpt_crosscheck.py` into `data/extract_v2/qa/gpt_check_2026-09_scores.md` and
`gpt_check_2026-09_wave2_scores.md`.

**Three agreement figures circulate for this cross-check, and they are not alternatives — they are
different scopes.** Read them as follows.

| scope | rows | studies | blind agreement | Cohen's kappa | fact-check confirmed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wave 1 (the first 106 studies extracted) | 313 | 106 | 81.8% | **0.732** | 79.9% |
| Wave 2 (the main-pool studies wave 1 had not reached) | 155 | 78 | 91.6% | **0.859** | 89.7% |
| **Pooled — the figure the manuscript reports (§4.8)** | **468** | **184 extracted, covering every main-pool study** | **85.0%** | **0.77** | **83.1%** |

Coverage was 148 of 148 main-pool studies when the waves were scored. §3 counts 147 because the
2026-09-04 rebuild of `apply_dispute_rulings.py` moved four rows to the appendix pool, and one study
had no main row left. The manuscript's §4.8 table quotes the 148 the cross-check was scored against.

Wave 1 was built when only 106 papers had been extracted, and only 70 of those ended in the main
pool, so most of the repair's frozen studies had no independent verification; reporting 0.732 as the
repair's reliability would have been a kappa over less than half the new corpus. Wave 2 closed that
gap. Wave 2 scores better than wave 1 because the R1-R10 rulings and the wave-1 adjudications had
already removed the row classes that generated most of the first wave's disputes. The pooled kappa
0.77 sits against the 0.802 the pre-repair corpus carries from its own independent-model check: the
repair's new material is verified to the same standard as the material it joins, by a different
model family, with full coverage of what entered the freeze.

The fact-check percentages are error rates, not agreement statistics — the coder saw our answer, so
they cannot be turned into a kappa. Pooled, the 79 non-confirmed rows are 24 wrong construct, 17 not
found in the paper, 16 wrong value, 16 paper not eligible, 6 wrong denominator.

**The disagreements are not scattered.** In wave 1, 23 rows where we say content and the blind coder
says quality, and 18 where we say recall and it says sharing. Both are boundaries the extraction
agents had already flagged themselves, so the cross-check independently located the same two soft
spots.

**Our own borderline flag predicts the disagreements.** In wave 1, rows we did not flag were
confirmed 91% of the time and rows we flagged borderline 55%; in wave 2, 95% against 76%. The flag
carries real information rather than decorating the file.

Every disagreement went to adjudication against the papers — 104 rows from wave 1, 23 from wave 2.
No verdict is applied automatically. Each is read against its paper and ruled ours, theirs, or a
third reading, with the reason recorded. That standing rule exists because a disagreement is
evidence of a question, not evidence of an error, and applying the second coder's verdict blindly
would replace one unaudited judgement with another. Wave 1: OURS 81, THEIRS 9, THIRD 5, DROP_ROW 9
(78% of our disputed rows upheld on a re-read), applied by `scripts/apply_dispute_rulings.py`. That
round also caught a polarity bug none of our own checks had: nine rows across five studies stored
the paper's *accuracy* share in `value_pct` with only a free-text note about it, which read as
prevalence would have entered a pooled median. Wave 2: the author ruled 21 of 23 — OURS 9, DROP 12,
THIRD 2 — applied by `scripts/apply_wave2_rulings.py`, which is the v1.7.15 -> v1.7.16 step.

## 6. The work list, and how it was closed

`data/extract_v2/repair_2026-09/POST_CAMPAIGN_CHECKS.md` was the running list of what had to be
settled before the dataset could be re-frozen, and it is preserved as written. Every item on it was
closed before the freeze:

- **Class-level questions** (accuracy-side values; quality-versus-content typing across the
  LLM-benchmark papers; denominators counting ratings rather than answers; adversarial red-team
  prompt sets; mean counts of researcher-supplied claims) — settled by the author's rulings R1-R10,
  applied by `scripts/apply_repair_rulings.py`; effects in `rulings_effect.md`.
- **The two thin cells** (EXPOSURE k=2, SHARING's platform mix) — resolved 2026-09-04 by reading the
  papers. Both EXPOSURE rows were retyped OTHER and moved to the appendix, so EXPOSURE stays as
  frozen; SHARING turned out to be a denominator effect and is reported stratified (§4).
- **Quote integrity** — 20 ellipsis-joined quotes repaired, the validator rewritten to check the
  whole quote, reject ellipsis joins outright, accept either PDF rendering and normalise
  line-break hyphenation and ligatures. Final run: 339 papers, 0 problems.
- **The cross-check adjudication queues** — 104 rows from wave 1 and 23 from wave 2, all ruled
  against the papers (§5).
- **Risk of bias** — re-run over the whole corpus after the merge; RoB coverage equals included
  studies, which `make_counts_crosswalk.py` asserts on every run.

## 7. Status

**The repair entered the freeze as v1.7.15 on 2026-09-04** (`scripts/apply_v1715.py`), adding the
314 rows / 147 studies whose `pool` is `main`; the 219 appendix rows and 21 dropped rows stayed out,
each with its `pool_reason` in `repair_estimates_final.csv`. No repair study id collided with the
existing corpus.

**Its disputes were then adjudicated into v1.7.16** on the same day
(`scripts/apply_wave2_rulings.py`): the author's 21 wave-2 rulings removed 14 rows and took 6
studies out of the corpus.

The released freeze is **v1.7.16 (975 estimates / 456 studies**, MD5
`13eb9d1a5626db4a69302da08e42dd47`, git tag `dataset-frozen-v1.7.16`). `docs/FROZEN.md` is the
authoritative pointer and `docs/counts_crosswalk.md` defines what each count means; if this document
and those two ever disagree, they are right.
