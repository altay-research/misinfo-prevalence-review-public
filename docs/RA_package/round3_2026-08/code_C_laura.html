Coding rules (click to open — read once before starting)
The question. Read the full paper and decide: does it report a usable quantitative estimate of misinformation prevalence, audience exposure, observed sharing, reach, self-reported recall, or concentration — i.e. a proportion with a stated denominator (a % of items, of people, of visits, of shares, or a top-X% concentration share)? “Misinformation” is broad: misinformation, disinformation, fake/false news, unreliable/untrustworthy sources, junk news.

INCLUDE if such an estimate is reported from real behaviour, real content, or survey self-report — even if it is not the paper's main result.

EXCLUDE if the paper only has:
· stated sharing intentions or hypothetical “would you share” designs;
· belief / accuracy-rating / susceptibility outcomes (who believes what);
· a detection model's performance (precision/recall of a classifier, not human exposure);
· intervention / correction effects only;
· purely qualitative analysis, or numbers with no stated denominator;
· quality/usefulness ratings only (DISCERN-style scores are not a misinformation share).

A few papers may be partly non-English; judge from the English abstract and any translated sections, and say so in your reason.

Blind rules. Don't consult the dataset; don't discuss items with the other coder until both are finished. Notes are optional; a word on borderline calls helps the adjudication of disagreements.
