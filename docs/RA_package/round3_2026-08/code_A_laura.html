The task. You are re-screening titles for a review of the prevalence and concentration of misinformation exposure. “Misinformation” is broad: misinformation, disinformation, fake/false news, unreliable/untrustworthy websites or sources, junk or low-quality news. These titles are a random sample from the ~20,000 records returned by the review's broad keyword search (a Scopus Boolean query crossing misinformation terms with measurement terms, tuned for recall), so many are off-topic by design — that is the search working, not a trick. Judge each title on its own; do not keyword-match.

INCLUDE — the title clearly indicates a study that measures one of the following, so a quantitative estimate is plausibly reported in the paper:
· audience exposure to misinformation (share of diet, reach, impressions, views);
· consumption of misinformation (visits, time, views);
· observed real-world sharing / spread / dissemination of misinformation;
· concentration of any of the above (share by top X%, supersharers, Gini);
· actor-level prevalence (misinformation as a share of politicians'/outlets'/influencers' output);
· content prevalence (share of posts/items that are false/unreliable).

MAYBE — plausibly one of the above but the title is ambiguous, or you are unsure. When in doubt, choose MAYBE — never EXCLUDE a possibly-relevant study.

EXCLUDE — only if the title is clearly one of:
· computer-science detection / classification / algorithm / model method;
· intervention / correction / fact-check-effect / inoculation / nudge study;
· belief / susceptibility / perception-only (who believes), no exposure estimate;
· hypothetical or stated sharing intentions (“would you share” experiments);
· non-empirical: commentary, theory, book, editorial, proceedings volume, dataset-resource paper;
· clearly off-topic (unrelated clinical / marketing / education / library / engineering…).

Blind rules. Don't consult the dataset or the other coder until both are done. The sample mixes the pipeline's includes, maybes and excludes. A decision on every row beats a perfect subset.
